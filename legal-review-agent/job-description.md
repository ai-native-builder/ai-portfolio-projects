## About the Role

PortSwigger is going all-in on AI. Not as a side project, not as a slogan — as the central way they intend to work across every part of the business.

It's a role for a builder. You'll drop into hard, unscoped AI problems across the business, usually in the parts that haven't figured AI out yet. You'll embed alongside the people closest to the work for around two weeks, ship something that actually makes things better, then move on to the next problem. Roving, not tribe-aligned. Always in the work, never writing slides about it.

## About You

You're fanatical about AI. It's rewired how you live.

You wake up and check what dropped overnight — a model release, a new agent framework, a benchmark, a product launch — and if something interesting landed, half your morning's gone to playing with it. Your weekend project is the next tool you wanted that nobody had built yet. Your home is full of little things you've stitched together for yourself: the budget tracker, the weekly meal planner, the shopping list that re-orders itself, the bedtime story generator.

You have personal subscriptions to several AI products and you've played with many more. Within hours of a new one dropping you're inside it, and you have a real opinion by the end of the week about what it's actually good at.

You don't just talk about AI — you build with it. You've taken an ambiguous brief, with no spec and no team, and produced a working thing under your own steam. Recently. You measured whether it actually helped, because you're as interested in the evals as you are in the build. You leave an artefact trail — repos, prototypes, write-ups, side projects — and that's how we'll get to know how you think.

You travel light across domains. Drop you into legal, marketing, customer success, recruitment, operations, finance, data — and you see the agentic opportunity quickly. You work alongside the domain expert, not above them.

## What You'll Do

Two-week sprints, each in a different corner of the business:

- Walk into a corner of the business that hasn't cracked AI yet and spot the agentic opportunity
- Sit alongside the people closest to the work and learn, fast, how they actually operate
- Design, build and ship a real thing — not a deck, not a strategy, an artefact someone uses on Monday morning
- Evaluate it properly: did this actually help, by how much, against what
- Help the team adopt what works — then move to the next domain entirely
- Build a sharp, opinionated take on which AI tools are actually best for what, and share it back across the org

Example sprint: embedded with the legal team for a fortnight — shadowing a contract review, finding where the friction is, building a working AI thing that saves a lawyer hours, evaluating it properly, and handing it over so it keeps running when you leave.
